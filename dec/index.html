Dec Measurement System

Introducing Dec, a measurement system, which uses turns instead of months, weeks, hours, minutes, seconds, and degrees.

0 Dec measurement system

This part of my website focuses on Dec, a measurement system that I created. All Dec measurements are based on turns. In Dec, turns can be combined metric prefixes, such as deci, centi, or milli, and can have different names depending on what they measure. For example, Dec measures longitude in wěi (w), latitude in jīng (j), and compass🧭directions in roses🌹(r).

2 Distance speed duration

Dec measures distance or length in taur (c), speed in omegar (v), and time in years (y) and days (d). Each of these four measurement units is based on a physical property of the planet Earth🌍: c on its circumference, v on the speed of its rotation at the Equator (0 mj), y on the duration of its orbit around the Sun️, and d on the duration of its rotation on its axis.

At a speed of 0.5 v or 500 milliomegar (mv), we could travel the 0.1 c or 100 millitaur (mc) between the default positions📍of Points 0 and 1 in 0.2 d or 200 millidays (md). The current distance between Points 0 and 1 is mc or c and the current travel time between the two points is mc ÷ v = md = c ÷ v = mc ÷ mv = d.

5 Zone equatorial meter (zem)

Apart from c, Dec also measures distance using a unit called the zone equatorial meter (zem). The width of a Dec time zone at the Equator is approximately ten million (~107) zem (z). Similarly, the distance from the Equator to one of the Poles is ~107 SI meters (sim). In other words, a decijīng (dj) is ~107 z long and a quarter meridian is ~107 sim long: 1 z = 0.4 sim.

7 Typical seat height

According to dimensions.com, 115 centizem (cz) is the typical seat height for both men and women in the age range of 25 to 45 y. The centi prefix means a hundredth (10-2). A box📦or cube pouf that is the size of a cubic zem (z³) would likely fit under a typical chair or in between the shins of two people sitting in front of each other with their knees and feet 1 z apart.

8 Perpetually setting sun

In Slovak🇸🇰, zem means Earth. This is fitting because all Dec units introduced above are derived from physical attributes of the Earth. At the Equator, the Earth rotates on its axis at a speed of about 1.00224 v. If we could indefinitely maintain this speed while flying West in an airplane✈️towards the setting sun️, we would be able to perpetually fly into the sunset🌅.

9 Airplane cruising speed

To travel fast enough for a perpetual sunset, we would need to be able to sustain supersonic flight. The speed of sound🔊(sos) at 15° Celsius and 1 standard atmosphere is roughly 0.735048 v or Mach 1. Mach numbers are relative to the sos, which varies greatly by air temperature and pressure. The cruising speed of a Boeing 747 is about 0.54 v or roughly Mach 0.85.

The highway🛣️speed of a car🚗is approximately tenfold slower than the cruising speed of an airplane️. If we are driving on a highway at a speed of 50 mv and our exit is 1000 z away, we will have 20 centimillidays (cmd) until we have to exit the highway️. 10 Centimilliday (cmd)

Dec refers to cmd as beats (b) because they are similar in duration to heart❤️beats or musical beats. In Dec, 1 d can be split into 100 centiday (cd) or 100000 (105) b and 1 mc can be divided into 100 kilozem (kz) or 105 z. The kilo prefix means a thousand (103). By changing prefixes and units, we can adapt mv for different magnitudes: mv = mc/d = kz/cd = z/b.

Since 1924, the distance of an Olympic marathon has been 1.054875 mc. Dec defines a marathon to be 1 mc, which is the original definition of a marathon from the first modern Olympic games in 1896. The 2026 world record marathon time is about 83 md. If we ran 1 mc in 83 md, our mean slowness would be 83 b per kz and our mean speed would be roughly 12.0482 mv.

A brisk walk🚶is about four times slower than a world record marathon. If we walk at 3 mv, or 4.8 kilometers per Dec hour, and each of our Dec steps is 2 z long, we take 1.5 steps per b or 100 steps per Dec minute, a typical walking cadence. Our mean slowness would be about 333 b per kz, each Dec mile would take 2000 steps and 20 Dec minutes, and the popular daily goal of 10000 steps would cover 5 Dec miles in 100 Dec minutes or about 67 md.

11 Heart rate tempo

A normal resting heart rate is between 86.4 and 144 per md (‰). The unofficial anthem of the Dec measurement system, “Turn the beat around”, has a tempo of 188.64 ‰, which corresponds to the allegro marking. A Dec clock⏰ticks at a rate of 100 ‰, b-1, 1/b, 1 inverse beat, or 1 perbeat (þ), which is 10/9 times more frequent than once per Dec second (s).

12 Frequency period wavelength

Dec uses þ, b, and z, often with metric prefixes, to measure the frequency, period, and wavelength, respectively, of a sound or light wave. Equations 1, 2, and 3 below show how frequency, period, and wavelength are related to each other and to speed. The speed of light (sol) is around 647551.709 v, which is about 880965 times faster than the sos.

\[\text{frequency} = \dfrac{\text{speed}}{\text{wavelength}} = \dfrac{1}{\text{period}} \tag{1}\]

\[\text{period} = \dfrac{\text{wavelength}}{\text{speed}} = \dfrac{1}{\text{frequency}} \tag{2}\]

\[\text{wavelength} = \text{speed} \times \text{period} = \dfrac{\text{speed}}{\text{frequency}} \tag{3}\]

The approximate frequency limits of humans senses are 328.32 to 682.56 trillion þ for the visible light and 10.368 to 24192 þ for audible sound. 15 Multiplicative inverse pair (mip)

Dec classifies b and þ as a “multiplicative inverse pair” (mip). We can measure period in b or d, frequency in þ or inverse days called quotidie (q), speed in v, and slowness in inverse v called upsilonr (ʌ). Whereas d and v are turns (T), q and ʌ are inverse turns (ꓕ). The frequency of a cd is 100 q or 0.01 þ. A speed of 10 mv or 0.01 v corresponds with a slowness of 100 ʌ.

16 Multiples and submultiples

Table 3 below lists the names, symbols, and ꓕ values of Dec turn submultiples. Outside of Dec, minutes (m) and seconds (s) are parts of a day or a degree and are used for both angular and time measurement. Dec extends this flexibility to hours (h) by redefining h, m, and s as turn submultiples. In Dec, 1 h is 4 centiturns (¢), 1 m is 2/3 ₥, and 1 s is 10/9 b.

In Dec, bǎi means hectoturn and xún means decaturn. In Korean, 100 can be written as 백 using Hangul or as 百 using Hanja. Whereas the symbol for xún (x) is derived from Pinyin, the symbol for bǎi (ﾲ) is from Hangul. Dec uses ﾲ, the first letter in 백, as the symbol for bǎi, because it can, unlike 백 or 百, match the width of all of the other symbols in Table 4.

The names in Tables 3 and 4 can be used on their own, just as kilo could refer to a kilogram outside of Dec. A kilogram is 1000 grams and a kebigram is 1024 grams, but Dec splits a kilogram into 15625 cubic cz (cz3) and a kebigram into 16000 cz3. In cz3, a kilogram cannot be evenly halved and a kebigram can be evenly halved seven times: 16000 = 125 × 27.

In Dec, 1 astronomical unit (AU) is 3.74 kilotaur (kc). For astronomy, Dec defines a new unit called a “parallax microturn” (parmic) as 3.74 gigataur / 𝜏. A parmic is roughly equal to 2.51667 light years, 919.22 light days, or 595.239 megataur. A light beat is about 6.4755 c, which means that light can go around the Earth roughly 6.5 times per b.

The average distance from the Earth to the Moon is about 9.6 c. Light can travel twice that distance in roughly 3 b, but would need about 3 md for the shortest possible round trip between Earth and Venus. The approximate distance from Earth to Proxima Centauri b, the nearest potentially habitable planet, is 1 gigataur, 1.68 parmic, or 4.228 light years.

20 Miles per hour

When we divide a 1D unit by a time unit, we obtain a speed unit. A Dec mile per h is exactly one mv. We can also equate an mv with microtaur (μc) per md, or nanotaur (nc) per microday (μd), or ell (ℓ) per s: mv = μc/md = nc/μd = ℓ/s, where micro is a millionth, nano is a billionth, a μc is a bǎizem (ﾲz), an nc is a decizem (dz), and an ℓ is a cubit or 10/9 z.

23 Grain pound ton

Dec and SI measurements of mass are based on volumes of water🌊. A dz³ of water weighs \(1\over7\) Dec pounds, 64 grams, or 1000 Dec grains (g). Similarly, u originates from uncia, the Latin word for “a twelfth”.

Summary

This article introduces the Dec measurement system and describes how Dec uses the properties of the planet Earth to define units based on turns for geographic coordinates, compass directions, distances, speeds, areas, volumes, weights, frequencies, wavelengths, and periods. Dec units can be combined with metric prefixes to form multiples or submultiples.

Dec attempts to bridge the gap, improve interoperability, and facilitate conversion between the US customary and SI measurement systems by redefining certain units. Redefinition of US customary units makes most human-based length units ~1.58% shorter and surveying distance units ~0.58% shorter. Dec also redefines SI seconds (sis) to be 4% shorter.

Dec color labels can convey an impression of a value at a glance👀. Dec sound labels allow us to quickly estimate a value without even having to look at it🙈. Both types of labels can help avoid confusion when decimal separators appear, disappear, or move due to a measurement unit change such as the addition, removal, or replacement of a metric prefix.

This part of my website focuses on Dec, a [measurement system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_of_units_of_measurement#:~:text=a%20collection%20of%20units%20of%20measurement%20and%20rules%20relating%20them%20to%20each%20other) that [I](https://maptv.github.io) created. All Dec measurements are based on [turns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turn_%28angle%29#:~:text=a%20unit%20of%20plane%20angle%20measurement%20equal%20to%202%CF%80%C2%A0radians%2C%20360%C2%A0degrees). In Dec, turns can be combined [metric prefixes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_prefix#:~:text=a%20unit%20prefix%20that%20precedes%20a%20basic%20unit%20of%20measure%20to%20indicate%20a%20multiple%20or%20submultiple%20of%20the%20unit), such as [deci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deci-#:~:text=a%20decimal%20unit%20prefix%20in%20the%20metric%20system%20denoting%20a%20factor%20of%20one%20tenth), [centi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centi-#:~:text=a%20unit%20prefix%20in%20the%20metric%20system%20denoting%20a%20factor%20of%20one%20hundredth), or [milli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milli-#:~:text=a%20unit%20prefix%20in%20the%20metric%20system%20denoting%20a%20factor%20of%20one%20thousandth), and can have different names depending on what they measure. For example, Dec measures [longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitude#:~:text=denoted%20by%20the%20Greek%20letter%20lambda) in [wěi](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E7%B7%AF#:~:text=(geography)-,latitude,-coordinate%20terms%C2%A0%E2%96%B2) ([w](#w){#wei .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="wěi"}), [latitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latitude#:~:text=denoted%20by%20the%20Greek%20lower%2Dcase%20letter%20phi) in [jīng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meridian_arc#Full_meridian_(polar_perimeter):~:text=The%20polar%20Earth%27s%20circumference%20is%20simply%20four%20times%20quarter%20meridian) ([j](#j){#jing .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="jīng"}), and [compass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass#:~:text=a%20device%20that%20shows%20the%20cardinal%20directions%20used%20for%20navigation%20and%20geographic%20orientation)🧭directions in [roses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_rose#:~:text=a%20polar%20diagram%20displaying%20the%20orientation%20of%20the%20cardinal%20directions)🌹([r](#r){#rose .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="roses"}).

# Longitude latitude course {.hiddenheading #sec-llc}

@tbl-map below⬇️provides the longitude in [milliwěi]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="thousandths of a wěi"} ([mw](#mw){#milliwei .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="thousandths of a wěi"}) and latitude in [millijīng]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="thousandths of a jīng"} ([mj](#mj){#millijing .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millijīng"}) of Points [0]{.point0} and [1]{.point1} on the [map](#fig-distmap)🗺️beneath @tbl-map. The milli prefix means a thousandth ([10^-3^]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="a thousandth"}). By default, Point [0]{.point0} is at [800]{.color8} [mw](#mw){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="thousandths of a wěi"} and [0]{.color0} [mj](#mj){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millijīng"}, near the [Galápagos🏝️archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gal%C3%A1pagos_Islands#:~:text=an%20archipelago%20of%20volcanic%20islands%20in%20the%20Eastern%20Pacific) of [Ecuador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecuador#:~:text=a%20country%20in%20northwestern%20South%20America)🇪🇨, and Point [1]{.point1} is at [800]{.color8} [mw](#mw){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="thousandths of a wěi"} and [100]{.color1} [mj](#mj){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millijīng"}, near the bottom of the [Missouri bootheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Bootheel#:~:text=a%20salient%20(protrusion)%20located%20in%20the%20southeasternmost%20part%20of%20the%20U.S.%20state%20of%20Missouri) in the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States#:~:text=a%20country%20primarily%20located%20in%20North%20America)🇺🇸.

Alongside the [geographic coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographic_coordinate_system#:~:text=positions%20directly%20on%20Earth%20as%20latitude%20and%20longitude) of one of the two points, each row of @tbl-map contains the [course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azimuth#:~:text=%20azimuth%20is%20usually%20denoted%20alpha) in [milliroses]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="thousandths of a compass rose"} ([mr](#mr){#millirose .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="milliroses"}) we would need to maintain to travel🧳the shortest path to the other point. The shortest path between the two points is shown as orange🟠dots on the [map](#fig-distmap). The default courses in [mr](#mr){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="milliroses"} are [0]{.color0} (North) from Point [0]{.point0} to [1]{.point1} and [500]{.color5} (South) from Point [1]{.point1} to [0]{.point0}.

To move the points, click the [map](#fig-distmap) or edit their coordinates in @tbl-map. The [toggle](https://observablehq.com/framework/inputs/toggle)✅inputs above⬆️@tbl-map add layers to the [map](#fig-distmap): country borders, a rainbow🌈colored grid of Dec [graticules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graticule_(cartography)#:~:text=a%20graphical%20depiction%20of%20a%20coordinate%20system%20as%20a%20grid%20of%20lines), a [choropleth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choropleth_map#:~:text=a%20type%20of%20statistical%20thematic%20map%20that%20uses%20pseudocolor) of [Coordinated Universal Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinated_Universal_Time#:~:text=the%20primary%20time%20standard%20globally%20used%20to%20regulate%20clocks%20and%20time) ([UTC](#utc){#coordinateduniversaltime .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="Coordinated Universal Time"}) time zones, and a [solar terminator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminator_(solar)#:~:text=a%20moving%20line%20that%20divides%20the%20daylit%20side%20and%20the%20dark%20night%20side%20of%20a%20planetary%20body) with a yellow🟡dot denoting the [point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsolar_point#:~:text=the%20point%20at%20which%20its%20Sun%20is%20perceived%20to%20be%20directly%20overhead) where the Sun☀️is [directly overhead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenith#:~:text=the%20imaginary%20point%20on%20the%20celestial%20sphere%20directly%20%22above%22%20a%20particular%20location): \${sunLonHsl} [mw](#mw){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="thousandths of a wěi"} and \${sunLatHsl} [mj](#mj){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millijīng"}.

# Distance speed duration {.hiddenheading #sec-dsd}

Dec measures distance or length in [taur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turn_(angle)#Tau_proposals:~:text=%E2%81%A0%20turn-,Circumference%20of%20a%20circle,-%F0%9D%90%B6) ([c](#c){#taur .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="taur"}), speed in [omegar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angular_velocity#:~:text=linear%20velocity%20is%20the%20radius%20times%20the%20angular%20velocity) ([v](#v){#omegar .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="omegar"}), and time in years ([y](#y){#year .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="years"}) and days ([d](#d){#day .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="days"}). Each of these four measurement units is based on a physical property of the planet Earth🌍: [c](#c){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="taur"} on its [circumference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth%27s_circumference#:~:text=the%20distance%20around%20Earth), [v](#v){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="omegar"} on the speed of its rotation at the [Equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator#:~:text=the%20circle%20of%20latitude%20that%20divides%20Earth%20into%20the%20Northern%20and%20Southern%20hemispheres) ([0]{.color0} [mj](#mj){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millijīng"}), [y](#y){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="year"} on the duration of its [orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth%27s_orbit#:~:text=From%20a%20vantage%20point%20above%20the%20north%20pole%20of%20either%20the%20Sun%20or%20Earth%2C%20Earth%20would%20appear%20to%20revolve%20in%20a%20counterclockwise%20direction%20around%20the%20Sun) around the Sun️, and [d](#d){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="day"} on the duration of its [rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth%27s_rotation#:~:text=the%20rotation%20of%20planet%20Earth%20around%20its%20own%20axis) on its [axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_tilt#:~:text=the%20imaginary%20line%20that%20passes%20through%20both%20the%20north%20pole%20and%20south%20pole).

At a speed of [0.5]{.color5} [v](#v){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="omegar"} or [500]{.color5} [milliomegar]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="thousandths of an omegar"} ([mv](#mv){#milliomegar .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="milliomegar"}), we could travel the [0.1]{.color1} [c](#c){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="taur"} or [100]{.color1} [millitaur]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="thousandths of a taur"} ([mc](#mc){#millitaur .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millitaur"}) between the default positions📍of Points [0]{.point0} and [1]{.point1} in [0.2]{.color2} [d](#d){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="days"} or [200]{.color2} [millidays]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="thousandths of a day"} ([md](#md){#milliday .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millidays"}). The current distance between Points [0]{.point0} and [1]{.point1} is \${distance_mcHsl()} [mc](#mc){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millitaur"} or \${distance_cHsl()} [c](#c){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="taur"} and the current travel time between the two points is \${distance_mcHsl()} [mc](#mc){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millitaur"} &divide; \${velocity_vHsl()} [v](#v){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="omegar"} = \${traveltimeHsl0} [md](#md){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millidays"} = \${distance_cHsl()} [c](#c){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="taur"} &divide; \${velocity_vHsl()} [v](#v){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="omegar"} = \${distance_mcHsl()} [mc](#mc){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millitaur"} &divide; \${velocity_mvHsl} [mv](#mv){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="milliomegar"} = \${traveltimeHsl1} [d](#d){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="days"}.

## Color wheel compass {.hiddenheading .unnumbered #cwc}

The [color🎨wheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_wheel#:~:text=an%20abstract%20illustrative%20organization%20of%20color%20hues%20around%20a%20circle) compass above indicates both a [hue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hue#:~:text=an%20angular%20position%20around%20a%20central%20or%20neutral%20point%20or%20axis%20on%20a%20color%20space%20coordinate%20diagram) and a course in [milliturns]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="thousandths of a turn"} ([₥](#mt){#milliturn .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="milliturns"}). We can convert [₥](#mt){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="milliturns"} to [compass degrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass#:~:text=north%20corresponds%20to%200%C2%B0%2C%20and%20the%20angles%20increase%20clockwise%2C%20so%20east%20is%2090%C2%B0%2C%20south%20is%20180%C2%B0%2C%20and%20west%20is%20270%C2%B0) ([c°](#cdeg){#compassdegree .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="compass degrees"}): 25 [₥](#mt){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="milliturns"} = 9[°](#deg){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="degrees"}, but the conversion of [₥](#mt){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="milliturns"} to [hue degrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HSL_and_HSV#Hue_and_chroma:~:text=measured%20in%20degrees%20%5B0%C2%B0%2C%20360%C2%B0%29) ([h°](#hdeg){#huedegree .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="HSL or HSV degrees"}) requires a [piecewise function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piecewise_function#:~:text=a%20function%20whose%20domain%20is%20partitioned%20into%20several%20intervals). To rotate🔄the color wheel compass, use the "Hue" [range](https://observablehq.com/framework/inputs/range)🎚️and [hue bar](https://observablehq.com/@paavanb/progressive-color-picker) inputs beneath it or change the course from Point [0]{.point0} to [1]{.point1} on the [map](#fig-distmap) above.

# Red green blue (rgb) {.hiddenheading #sec-rgb}

Together, the range inputs underneath the hue bar form a "[hue saturation lightness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HSL_and_HSV#:~:text=HSL%20stands%20for-,hue%2C%20saturation%2C%20and%20lightness,-%2C%20and%20is%20often)" ([HSL](#hsl){#huesaturationlightness .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="hue saturation lightness"}) triplet. Like "[[red]{.color0} [green]{.color4} [blue]{.color7}](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RGB_color_model#:~:text=an%20additive%20color%20model)" ([RGB](#rgb){#redgreenblue .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="red green blue"}) or [hexadecimal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_colors#Hex_triplet:~:text=hexadecimal%20number%20used%20in%20HTML%2C%20CSS%2C%20SVG%2C%20and%20other%20computing%20applications%20to%20represent%20colors) ([hex](#hex){#hexadecimal .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="hexadecimal"}) triplets, [HSL](#hsl){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="hue saturation lightness"} triplets specify a full-fledged color instead of just a hue.
Beneath the hue bar and range inputs, @tbl-color compares the course from Point [0]{.point0} to Point [1]{.point1} in its top row with the [cardinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_direction#:~:text=north%2C%20south%2C%20east%2C%20and%20west) and [intercardinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_direction#:~:text=northeast%20(NE)%2C%20southeast%20(SE)%2C%20southwest%20(SW)%2C%20and%20northwest%20(NW)) directions.

Dec color (Decolor) labels🏷️provide a general idea of angular [measure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle#:~:text=The%20magnitude%20of%20an%20angle), regardless of the metric prefixes or measurement [units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle#Units) we use. Therefore, we can reuse♻️Decolors across many different contexts. With the exception of latitude, [red]{.color0} designates starting points, like North ([0]{.color0} [r](#r){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="roses"}) and midnight ([0]{.color0} [d](#d){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="days"}), and [cyan]{.color5} denotes midpoints, such as South ([0.5]{.color5} [r](#r){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="roses"}) and noon ([0.5]{.color5} [d](#md){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="days"}).

# Dec time zones {.hiddenheading #sec-dtz}

Enable the ["Grid" toggle input](#toggles) above @tbl-map to see Latitudes [-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/72nd_parallel_south#:~:text=a%20circle%20of%20latitude%20that%20is%2072%20degrees%20south%20of%20the%20Earth's%20equatorial%20plane%20in%20the%20Antarctic) ([-200]{.color8} [mj](#mj){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millijīng"}), [-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/36th_parallel_south#:~:text=a%20circle%20of%20latitude%20that%20is%2036%20degrees%20south%20of%20the%20Earth's%20equatorial%20plane) ([-100]{.color9} [mj](#mj){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millijīng"}), [0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator#:~:text=the%20circle%20of%20latitude%20that%20divides%20Earth%20into%20the%20Northern%20and%20Southern%20hemispheres) ([0]{.color0} [mj](#mj){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millijīng"}), [1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/36th_parallel_north#:~:text=a%20circle%20of%20latitude%20that%20is%2036%20degrees%20north%20of%20the%20Earth's%20equatorial%20plane) ([100]{.color1} [mj](#mj){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millijīng"}), and [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/72nd_parallel_north#:~:text=a%20circle%20of%20latitude%20that%20is%2072%20degrees%20north%20of%20the%20Earth's%20equatorial%20plane%2C%20in%20the%20Arctic) ([200]{.color2} [mj](#mj){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millijīng"}) on the [map](#fig-distmap) above along with the ten major lines of longitude that divide the Earth🌎into the ten Dec time zones. In Dec, [Longitude 0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/18th_meridian_west#:~:text=a%20line%20of%20longitude%20that%20extends%20from%20the%20North%20Pole%20across%20the%20Arctic%20Ocean%2C%20Greenland%2C%20Iceland%2C%20the%20Atlantic%20Ocean%2C%20the%20Canary%20Islands%2C%20the%20Southern%20Ocean%2C%20and%20Antarctica%20to%20the%20South%20Pole) ([0]{.color0} [mw](#mw){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="thousandths of a wěi"}) serves as the [Prime Meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_meridian#:~:text=an%20arbitrarily%2Dchosen%20meridian%20%28a%20line%20of%20longitude%29%20in%20a%20geographic%20coordinate%20system%20at%20which%20longitude%20is%20defined%20to%20be%200%C2%B0), [International Date Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Date_Line#:~:text=the%20line%20between%20the%20South%20and%20North%20Poles%20that%20is%20the%20boundary%20between%20one%20calendar%20day%20and%20the%20next), the start of Zone [0]{.color0}, and the end of Zone [9]{.color9}.

Like the ten major lines of longitude that separate them, the ten Dec time zones are numbered [0]{.color0} to [9]{.color9}. The integer assigned to each time zone is its "time zone offset" ([tzo]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="time zone offset"}). Dec measures [tzo]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="time zone offsets"} in single-digit, non-negative [decidays]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="tenths of a day"} ([dd](#dd){#deciday .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="decidays"}). # Perpetually setting sun {.hiddenheading #sec-pss}

In [Slovak](https://sk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zem)🇸🇰, [zem](#z){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="zone equatorial meter"} means Earth. This is fitting because all Dec units introduced above are derived from physical attributes of the Earth. At the Equator, the Earth rotates on its axis at a speed of about 1.00224 [v](#v){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="omegar"}. If we could indefinitely maintain this speed while flying West in an airplane✈️towards the setting sun️, we would be able to perpetually fly [into the sunset](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/RidingIntoTheSunset)🌅.

# Airplane cruising speed {.hiddenheading #sec-acs}

To travel fast enough for a perpetual sunset, we would need to be able to [sustain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercruise#:~:text=sustained%20supersonic%20flight) [supersonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersonic_speed#:~:text=the%20speed%20of%20an%20object%20that%20exceeds%20the%20speed%20of%20sound) flight. The [speed of sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_of_sound#:~:text=the%20distance%20travelled%20per%20unit%20of%20time%20by%20a%20sound%20wave)🔊([sos](#sos){#speedofsound .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="speed of sound"}) at 15[°](#deg){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="degrees"} [Celsius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celsius#:~:text=the%20unit%20of%20temperature%20on%20the%20Celsius%20temperature%20scale) and 1 [standard atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_atmosphere_(unit)#:~:text=a%20unit%20of%20pressure%20defined%20as%20101325%20Pa) is roughly 0.735048 [v](#v){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="omegar"} or Mach 1. [Mach numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mach_number) are relative to the [sos](#sos){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="speed of sound"}, which varies greatly by air temperature and pressure. The [cruising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise_%28aeronautics%29#:~:text=phase%20of%20aircraft%20flight%20from%20when%20the%20aircraft%20levels%20off%20after%20a%20climb) speed of a [Boeing 747](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747#:~:text=sweep%2C%20allowing%20a-,Mach%C2%A00.85,-%28490%C2%A0kn;%20900) is about 0.54 [v](#v){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="omegar"} or roughly Mach 0.85.

The highway🛣️speed of a car🚗is approximately tenfold slower than the cruising speed of an airplane️. If we are driving on a highway at a speed of 50 [mv](#mv){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="milliomegar"} and our exit is 1000 [z](#z){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="zem"} away, we will have 20 [centimillidays]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="hundred thousandths of a day"} ([cmd](#cmd){#centimilliday .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="centimilliday"}) until we have to exit the highway️. To ensure we do not miss our exit, we can periodically check a countdown of the remaining [z](#z){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="zem"}: \${zLeft}.

# Centimilliday (cmd) {.hiddenheading #sec-cmd}

Dec refers to [cmd](#cmd){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="centimillidays"} as [beats]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="hundred thousandths of a day"} ([b](#b){#beat .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="beats"}) because they are similar in duration to heart❤️beats or [musical beats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beat_(music)#:~:text=I-,n%20music%20and%20music%20theory%2C%20the%20beat%20is%20the%20basic%20unit%20of%20time,-%2C%20the). In Dec, 1 [d](#d){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="day"} can be split into 100 [centiday]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="hundredths of a day"} ([cd](#cd){#centiday .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="centidays"}) or 100000 ([10^5^]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="a hundred thousand"}) [b](#b){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="beats"} and 1 [mc](#mc){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millitaur"} can be divided into 100 [kilozem]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="thousands of zem"} ([kz](#kz){#kilozem .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="thousands of zem"}) or [10^5^]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="a hundred thousand"} [z](#z){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="zem"}. The kilo prefix means a thousand ([10^3^]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="a thousand"}). By changing prefixes and units, we can adapt [mv](#mv){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="milliomegars"} for different [magnitudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnitude_%28mathematics%29#:~:text=a%20property%20which%20determines%20whether%20the%20object%20is%20larger%20or%20smaller%20than%20other%20objects%20of%20the%20same%20kind): [mv](#mv){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="milliomegar"} = [mc](#mc){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millitaur"}/[d](#d){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="day"} = [kz](#kz){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="a thousand of zem"}/[cd](#cd){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="centidays"} = [z](#z){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="zem"}/[b](#b){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="beats"}.

Since [1924]{.color24by100}, the distance of an Olympic marathon has been 1.054875 [mc](#mc){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millitaur"}. Dec defines a marathon to be 1 [mc](#mc){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millitaur"}, which is the [original definition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathon#Distance:~:text=Games%20were%20about-,40%20kilometres,-%2824.9%C2%A0mi) of a marathon from the [first modern Olympic games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1896_Summer_Olympics#:~:text=the%20first%20international%20Olympic%20Games%20held%20in%20modern%20history) in [1896]{.color96by100}. The [2026]{.color26by100} [world record marathon time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathon#Area_records:~:text=World-,1%3A59%3A30,2026,-Sabastian%20Sawe%C2%A0) is about [83]{.color083} [md](#md){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millidays"}. If we ran 1 [mc](#mc){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millitaur"} in [83]{.color083} [md](#md){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millidays"}, our mean [slowness](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/slowness#Noun:~:text=the%20reciprocal%20of%20velocity%2C%20that%20delineates%20the%20amount%20of%20time%20required%20for%20an%20object%20to%20travel%20a%20given%20distance) would be 83 [b](#b){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="beats"} per [kz](#kz){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="a thousand of zem"} and our mean speed would be roughly 12.0482 [mv](#mv){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="milliomegars"}.

A brisk walk🚶is about four times slower than a world record marathon. If we walk at 3 [mv](#mv){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="milliomegars"}, or 4.8 kilometers per Dec hour, and each of our Dec steps is 2 [z](#z){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="zem"} long, we take 1.5 steps per [b](#b){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="beat"} or 100 steps per Dec minute, a typical walking [cadence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadence_(gait)). # Frequency period wavelength {.hiddenheading #sec-fpw}

Dec uses [&thorn;](#per){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="perbeat"}, [b](#b){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="beats"}, and [z](#z){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="zem"}, often with metric prefixes, to measure the [frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency#:~:text=the%20number%20of%20occurrences%20of%20a%20repeating%20event%20per%20unit%20of%20time), [period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency#:~:text=the%20reciprocal%20of%20the%20frequency), and [wavelength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavelength#:~:text=the%20distance%20over%20which%20the%20wave%27s%20shape%20repeats), respectively, of a sound or light wave. Equations -@eq-freq, -@eq-peri, and -@eq-wave below show how frequency, period, and wavelength are related to each other and to speed. The speed of light ([sol](#sol){#speedoflight .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="speed of light"}) is around 647551.709 [v](#v){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="omegar"}, which is about 880965 times faster than the [sos](#sos){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="speed of sound"}.

:::{style="overflow-x:auto;overflow-y:hidden;"}
$$\text{frequency} = \dfrac{\text{speed}}{\text{wavelength}} = \dfrac{1}{\text{period}}$$ {#eq-freq}

$$\text{period} = \dfrac{\text{wavelength}}{\text{speed}} = \dfrac{1}{\text{frequency}}$$ {#eq-peri}

$$\text{wavelength} = \text{speed} \times \text{period} = \dfrac{\text{speed}}{\text{frequency}}$$ {#eq-wave}
:::

The approximate frequency limits of humans senses are 328.32 to 682.56 trillion [&thorn;](#per){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="perbeats"} for the  [visible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visible_spectrum#:~:text=the%20band%20of%20the%20electromagnetic%20spectrum%20that%20is%20visible%20to%20the%20human%20eye) light and [10.368](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hearing_range#:~:text=humans%20can%20hear%20sound%20as%20low%20as%2012%C2%A0Hz) to [24192](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hearing_range#:~:text=8%5D%20and-,as%20high%20as%2028%C2%A0kHz,-%2C%20though%20the%20threshold) [&thorn;](#per){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="perbeats"} for [audible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hearing_range#:~:text=the%20frequency%20range%20that%20can%20be%20heard%20by%20humans) sound. The range inputs below control the sound player beneath them. # Decioctave octave note {.hiddenheading #sec-don}

In addition to [&thorn;](#per){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="perbeats"}, we can also express frequencies in [octaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octave#:~:text=an%20interval%20between%20two%20notes%2C%20one%20having%20twice%20the%20frequency%20of%20vibration%20of%20the%20other) ([o](#o){#octave .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="octave"}), which are twofold changes (doublings) in frequency, or [decioctaves]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="tenths of an octave"} ([do](#do){#decioctave .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="decioctaves"}), which are tenths of an [o](#o){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="octave"}. The approximate frequency limits are [3]{.color3} to [103]{.color3} for human hearing and 23.76 to 3616.64 [&thorn;](#per){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="perbeats"} or [8]{.color8} to [80]{.color0} [do](#do){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="decioctaves"} for an 88-key piano🎹. # Multiplicative inverse pair (mip) {.hiddenheading #sec-mip}

Dec classifies [b](#b){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="a hundred-thousandth of a turn"} and [&thorn;](#per){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="perbeat"} as a "[multiplicative inverse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiplicative_inverse#:~:text=denoted%20by,x%E2%88%921) pair" ([mip](#mip){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="multiplicative inverse pair"}). We can measure period in [b](#b){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="beats"} or [d](#d){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="days"}, frequency in [&thorn;](#per){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="perbeats"} or inverse days called [quotidie]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="per days"} ([q](#q){#quotidie .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="quotidie"}), speed in [v](#v){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="omegar"}, and slowness in inverse [v](#v){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="omegar"} called upsilonr ([ʌ](#ur){#upsilonr .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="upsilonr"}). Whereas [d](#d){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="days"} and [v](#v){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="omegar"} are turns ([T](#T){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="turns"}), [q](#q){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="quotidie"} and [ʌ](#ur){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="upsilonr"} are inverse turns ([ꓕ](#inverset){#inverseturn .big .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="inverse turn"}). The frequency of a [cd](#cd){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="centidays"} is 100 [q](#q){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="quotidie"} or 0.01 [&thorn;](#per){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="perbeats"}. A speed of 10 [mv](#mv){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="milliomegar"} or 0.01 [v](#v){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="omegar"} corresponds with a slowness of 100 [ʌ](#ur){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="upsilonr"}.

$$\textsf{þ} = \textsf{b}^{\text{-1}} = \dfrac{1}{\textsf{b}}$$ {#eq-b}

$$\textsf{q} = \textsf{d}^{\text{-1}} = \dfrac{1}{\textsf{d}}$$ {#eq-d}

$$\textsf{ʌ} = \textsf{v}^{\text{-1}} = \dfrac{1}{\textsf{v}} = \dfrac{\textsf{d}}{\textsf{c}} = \dfrac{\textsf{b}}{\textsf{kz}}$$ {#eq-v}

$$\style{display:inline-block;transform:rotate(180deg)}{\text{T}} = \text{T}^{\text{-1}} = \dfrac{1}{\text{T}}$$ {#eq-t}

A light beat is about 6.4755 [c](#c){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="taur"}, which means that light can go around the Earth roughly 6.5 times per [b](#b){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="beat"}.

The average distance from the Earth to the Moon is about 9.6 [c](#c){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="taur"}. # Summary {.unnumbered #tldr}

This article introduces the Dec measurement system and describes how Dec uses the properties of the planet Earth to define units based on turns for geographic coordinates, compass directions, distances, speeds, areas, volumes, weights, frequencies, wavelengths, and periods. Dec units can be combined with metric prefixes to form multiples or submultiples.

The main benefit of Dec is consistency. Every Dec unit is a turn or a fraction of a turn, so one number can describe a course, a hue, a time of day, or a longitude, and one Decolor or sound label works for all of them. Taur, omegar, and days all come from the Earth's size and rotation, so distance, speed, and duration fit together without conversion factors. 1 [mv](#mv){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="milliomegar"} is 1 [mc](#mc){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millitaur"} per [d](#d){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="day"}, 1 [kz](#kz){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="thousand zem"} per [cd](#cd){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="centiday"}, or 1 [z](#z){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="zem"} per [b](#b){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="beat"}, so changing prefixes rescales a trip from a flight down to a footstep.

Powers of ten take the place of the 60s, 24s, and 360s in hours, minutes, seconds, and degrees. Dec combines months, [dom](#dom){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="day of month"}, hours, minutes, and seconds into days, so it can show a datetime as a year followed by a single base-10 number. For example, the [ISO](#iso){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="International Organization for Standardization"} 8601 datetime 2026-12-25T18:00:00 (19 characters) becomes 2026+299.75000 (14 characters): Year 2026, [doy](#doy){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="day of year"} 299 plus 75000 [b](#b){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="beats"}. Dec uses fewer characters and only one radix, yet it is more precise, because a beat is shorter than a second.

A Dec [doy](#doy){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="day of year"} such as 299 can also stand on its own as a single number without a year. In contrast, [ISO](#iso){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="International Organization for Standardization"} 8601 does not allow a month and [dom](#dom){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="day of month"} without a year. The [HTML]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="Hypertext Markup Language"} [yearless date](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/dev/common-microsyntaxes.html#yearless-dates:~:text=A%20yearless%20date%20consists%20of%20a%20Gregorian%20month%20and%20a%20day%20within%20that%20month%2C%20but%20with%20no%20associated%20year) 03-04 can be confusing, because many parts of the world put the [dom](#dom){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="day of month"} before the month and would read it as April 3 instead of March 4.

A location's [tzo](#tzo){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="time zone offset"} is just the whole number of [deciwěi]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="tenths of a wěi"} ([dw](#dw){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="deciwěi"}) in its longitude, a single digit that is never negative. Many Dec units also match human scales: a [z](#z){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="zem"} is about the width of two hands laid side by side, and a [b](#b){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="beat"} is about as long as a heartbeat❤️.

Dec attempts to bridge the gap, improve interoperability, and facilitate conversion between the [US](#us){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="United States"} customary and [SI](#si){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="International System of Units"} measurement systems by redefining certain units. Redefinition of [US](#us){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="United States"} customary units makes most human-based length units [~]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="approximately"}[1.58]{.color0158}% shorter and surveying distance units [~]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="approximately"}[0.58]{.color0058}% shorter. Dec also redefines [SI](#si){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="International System of Units"} seconds ([sis](#sis){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="SI seconds"}) to be [4]{.color040}% shorter.

Dec color labels can convey an impression of a value at a glance👀. Dec sound labels allow us to quickly estimate a value without even having to look at it🙈. Both types of labels can help avoid confusion when [decimal separators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal_separator#:~:text=In%20English%2Dspeaking%20countries%2C%20the%20decimal%20point%20is%20usually%20a%20small%20dot%20%28.%29%20placed%20either%20on%20the%20baseline) appear, disappear, or move due to a measurement unit change such as the addition, removal, or replacement of a metric prefix.
