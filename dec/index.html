Dec Measurement System

Introducing Dec, a measurement system, which uses turns instead of months, weeks, hours, minutes, seconds, and degrees.

0 Dec measurement system

This part of my website focuses on Dec, a measurement system that I created. All Dec measurements are based on turns. When measuring angles📐, a turn (t) represents a full⭕️circle and equals 360 degrees (°) or \(\underline\tau\), 2𝜋, or 900ϡ radians (rad). Geographic coordinates and compass🧭directions are angles and thus can — and should — be measured in turns instead of rad or °.

1 Longitude latitude course

Dec measures longitude in wěi (w), latitude in jīng (j), and compass directions in roses🌹(r). To measure certain kinds of angles, Dec uses specific types of turns with distinct names like w, j, or r. All turn types can be combined with metric prefixes, like deci, centi, or milli, to create turn submultiples, such as deciturns (dt), centiturns (ct), or milliturns (mt).

By default, Point 0 is at 800 mw and 0 mj, near the Galápagos🏝️archipelago of Ecuador🇪🇨, and Point 1 is at 800 mw and 100 mj, near the bottom of the Missouri bootheel in the United States🇺🇸.

The shortest distance is shown as orange🟠dots on the map. The default courses in mr are 0 (North) from Point 0 to 1 and 500 (South) from Point 1 to 0.

2 Distance speed duration

Dec measures distance in taur (c), speed in omegar (v), and time in years (y) and days (d). Each of these four turn types approximates (\(\approx\)) a physical property of the Earth🌍: c = \(\underline{\tau r}\) \(\approx\) its circumference, y \(\approx\) the duration of its orbit around the Sun️, d \(\approx\) the duration of its rotation on its axis, and \(\text c\over\text d\) = v = \(\underline{\omega r}\) \(\approx\) the speed of its rotation at the Equator.

At a speed of 0.5 v or 500 milliomegar (mv), we could travel the 0.1 c or 100 millitaur (mc) between the default positions📍of Points 0 and 1 in 0.2 d or 200 millidays (md). The time required to travel between two points is the distance divided by the speed: mc ÷ v = md = c ÷ v = mc ÷ mv = d.

5 Zone equatorial meter (zem)

Wikimedia

Apart from c, Dec also measures distance using a unit called the zone equatorial meter (zem). The width of a Dec time zone at the Equator is approximately ten million (~107) zem (z). Similarly, the distance from the Equator to one of the Poles is ~107 meters. In other words, a decijīng (dj) is ~107 z long and a quarter meridian is ~107 meters long.

6 Length area volume

You can approximate a z using your hands🤲. With your palms flat on a table in front of you and the tips of your thumbs👍touching, the maximum distance between the tips of your pinkies is ~1 z. When you spread out the fingers on one hand✋or do the “call me”, “drink”, or “shaka”🤙gesture, your thumb and pinky tips are ~0.5 z apart.

7 Typical seat height

According to dimensions.com, 115 centizem (cz) is the typical seat height for both men and women in the age range of 25 to 45 y. A box📦that is the size of a cubic zem (z³) would likely fit under a typical chair or in between the shins of two people sitting in front of each other with their knees and feet 1 z apart and their legs🦵bent at right angles (25 ct).

8 Perpetually setting sun

In Slovak🇸🇰, zem means Earth. This is fitting because all Dec units are based on physical attributes of the Earth. At the Equator, the Earth rotates on its axis at a speed of ~1.00224 v. If we could indefinitely maintain this speed while flying West in an airplane✈️towards the setting sun️, we would be able to perpetually fly into the sunset🌅.

9 Airplane cruising speed

To travel fast enough for a perpetual sunset, the airplane would need to surpass the speed of sound🔊(sos), which at 15 ° Celsius and 1 standard atmosphere is 0.735048 v or Mach 1. Mach numbers are relative to the sos, which varies greatly by air temperature and pressure. The cruising speed of a Boeing 747 is ~0.54 v or Mach ~0.85.

The highway🛣️speed of a car🚗is roughly tenfold slower than the cruising speed of an airplane️. If we are driving on a highway at a speed of 50 mv and our exit is 1000 z away, we will have 20 centimillidays (cmd) until we have to exit the highway️. 10 Centimilliday (cmd)

Dec refers to cmd as beats (b) because they are similar in duration to heart❤️beats or musical beats. In Dec, 1 d = 100 centiday (cd) = 105 b = 106 microdays (µd), 1 mc = 100 kilozem (kz) = 105 z = 106 decizem (dz) = 106 nanotaur (nc), and therefore, 1 mv = \(\text{mc}\over\text d\) = \(\text {kz}\over\text {cd}\) = \(\text z\over\text b\) = \(\text {dz}\over\text{µd}\) = \(\text {nc}\over\text{µd}\). A cd is 96% of a quarter hour and a b is 86.4% of a second.

11 Heart rate tempo

A normal resting heart rate is between 100 and 166.♄ b per md (bpm). The unofficial anthem of the Dec measurement system, “Turn the beat around”, has a tempo of 188.64 bpm, which corresponds to the allegro tempo marking. A Dec clock⏰ticks at a rate of 100 bpm, \(\text b^{-1}\), \(1\over\text b\), 1 inverse beat, or 1 perbeat (þ), which is 1.15♄♄ times more frequent than a Hertz.

12 Frequency period wavelength

Dec uses þ, b, and z, often with metric prefixes, to measure the frequency, period, and wavelength, respectively, of a sound or light wave. Equations 1, 2, and 3 below show how frequency, period, and wavelength are related to each other and to speed. The speed of light (sol) is ~647.551657 kiloomegar (kv), which is ~881 thousand times faster than the sos.

\[\text{frequency} = \dfrac{\text{speed}}{\text{wavelength}} = \dfrac{1}{\text{period}} \tag{1}\]

\[\text{period} = \dfrac{\text{wavelength}}{\text{speed}} = \dfrac{1}{\text{frequency}} \tag{2}\]

\[\text{wavelength} = \text{speed} \ast \text{period} = \dfrac{\text{speed}}{\text{frequency}} \tag{3}\]

The frequency range of the visible spectrum of light is ~328.32 to ~682.56 teraperbeats (Tþ). The range of sound frequencies which can be audible👂for humans is ~10.368 to ~24192 þ. The period and wavelength that correspond to the frequency chosen by the range input below are 1000 ÷ þ = millibeats (mb) and 735.048 mv ÷ þ = z.

13 Decioctave octave note

In addition to þ, the limits of human hearing can also be expressed in decioctaves (do). A do is a tenth of an octave (o). An o is a two-fold change in frequency. The audible range for humans is ~3 to ~103 do. The range of an 88-key piano🎹is ~23.76 to ~3616.64 þ or ~8 to ~80 do. The equations and code below convert between o and þ.

\[\text{p} = 14.1275 \ast 2^{\text{o}} \tag{5}\]

16 US customary units

Dec redefines US customary units to facilitate conversion with SI and Dec units. The values in the first column of the tables below are approximate fold changes from original to redefined US customary units. A fold change of 1 means 0 change is required for alignment with the SI and Dec units shown in the second and third column, respectively.

Unlike Dec and SI, the US customary measurement system does not use metric prefixes to scale units by powers of ten. Redefined US customary units can serve as convenient reference points and provide intuitive names for certain fractions and multiples of Dec and SI units. For example, after being redefined, one hand is equal to a decimeter or a quarter z.

17 Length and distance

The unidimensional (1D) units in Table 3 below can be divided into two groups: human- or horse-based length units and surveying distance units. A horse🐴length is about as long as a cruiser or touring motorcycle🏍️, approximately equivalent to the height of the tallest basketball🏀players, and roughly half of the length of a compact to mid-size car.

18 Miles per hour

When we divide a 1D unit by a time unit, we get a speed unit. A mile per hour is very close to a z per b and a knot, which is used to measure the speed of aircraft and watercraft, is almost exactly the same as 10/9 z per b. Dec refers to 10/9 z as a cubit or ell (ℓ). An mv can also be expressed as one ℓ per Dec second (s). There are 86400 SI seconds or 90000 s in one day.

19 Are hectare acre

If we raise a 1D unit to the second power, we get a bidimensional (2D) area unit. A z² is 1 hexamilliare, 16 square (sq.) decimeters (dm²), ~0.1975 Dec sq. yards, 1.♅ Dec sq. feet, or 256 Dec sq. inches. A sq. kilozem (kz²) is 1 hexakilare, 16 hectares, 1600 ares, 40 Dec acres, 0.16 sq. kilometers (km²), 0.0625 Dec sq. miles, 106 z², or 1 megahexamilliare.

20 Drop wineglass keg

US tridimensional (3D) volume units tend to scale by powers of two. A cubic (cu.) decizem (dz³) is like a double shot, either of espresso☕️or of liquor🥃, and is equal to 1 cu. nanotaur (nc³), 1 Dec wineglass (🍷glass), 2 Dec ounces (u), 4 Dec tablespoons (table🥄), 64 cu. centimeters (cm³), 64 milliliters (mL), 1000 Dec drops (g), or 1000 cu. centizem (cz³).

21 Grain pound ton

Dec and SI measurements of mass are based on volumes of water🌊. A dz³ of water weighs \(1\over7\) Dec pounds, 64 grams, or 1000 Dec grains (g). One u of water weighs \(1\over14\) Dec pounds, 500 Dec g, or 32 grams. In Dec, g is short for granum and gutta, the Latin words for grain and drop, respectively. Similarly, u originates from uncia, the Latin word for “a twelfth”.

22 Body mass index (bmi)

A z³ is 1 keg. A keg of water weighs 64 kilograms, 128 Dec pounds, or 1000 Dec kilograins (kg). If Leonardo da Vinci’s Vitruvian Man were 4 z tall, we could measure 1 z from his knees to his feet or from his elbows to his fingertips. If he also weighed 1000 Dec kg, his body mass index (BMI) would be 62.5 kg per z² (\(\text {kg}\over\text z^2\)) or 25 kilograms per m² (\(\text {kilogram}\over\text m^2\)).

An obese person has a BMI above 75 \(\text {kg}\over\text z^2\) or 30 \(\text {kg}\over\text m^2\). Severe or morbid obesity is defined as a BMI above 100 \(\text {kg}\over\text z^2\) or 40 \(\text {kg}\over\text m^2\). 24 Claude Boniface Collignon

In 1788, Claude Boniface Collignon proposed measuring length in dz or nc and tracking time in xún (x), dd, md, µd, and nanodays (nd)3. On 2025+039, while searching for units akin to c and z, I noticed the definition of a zem, 1 z = 10-8 c = 40 centimeters, in a table of ten possible length units from an arxiv article entitled “Why does the meter beat the second?”4.

Summary

This article introduces the Dec measurement system and describes how Dec uses the properties of the planet Earth to define units based on turns for geographic coordinates, compass directions, distances, speeds, areas, volumes, weights, frequencies, wavelengths, and periods. Dec units can be combined with metric prefixes to form multiples or submultiples.

Dec attempts to bridge the gap, improve interoperability, and facilitate conversion between the US customary and SI measurement systems by redefining certain units. Redefinition of US customary units makes human-based length units ~1.58% shorter and surveying distance units ~0.58% shorter. Dec also redefines SI seconds to be 4% shorter.

Dec color labels can convey an impression of a value at a glance👀. Dec sound labels allow us to quickly estimate a value without even having to look at it🙈. Both types of labels can help avoid confusion when decimal separators appear, disappear, or move due to a measurement unit change such as the addition, removal, or replacement of a metric prefix.

# Dec measurement system {.hiddenheading #sec-dec}

This part of my website focuses on Dec, a [measurement system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_of_units_of_measurement#:~:text=a%20collection%20of%20units%20of%20measurement%20and%20rules%20relating%20them%20to%20each%20other) that [I](https://maptv.github.io) created. All Dec measurements are based on [turns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turn_%28angle%29#:~:text=a%20unit%20of%20plane%20angle%20measurement%20equal%20to%202%CF%80%C2%A0radians%2C%20360%C2%A0degrees). When measuring [angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle#:~:text=the%20figure%20formed%20by%20two%20rays)📐, a [turn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turn_%28angle%29#:~:text=the%20Greek%20letter,to%20one%20turn) ([t](#t){#turn .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="turn"}) represents a full⭕️circle and equals 360 [degrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degree_(angle)#:~:text=a%20measurement%20of%20a%20plane%20angle%20in%20which%20one%20full%20rotation%20is%20360%20degrees) ([°](#deg){#degree .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="degrees"}) or [$\underline\tau$](#2pi){#tau .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="tau"}, 2[𝜋](#pi){#taudividedby2 .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="tau"}, or 900[ϡ](#sampi){#taudividedby900 .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="sampi"} [radians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radian#:~:text=the%20unit%20of%20angle%20in%20the%20International%20System%20of%20Units) ([rad](#rad){#radian .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="radians"}). [Geographic coordinates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographic_coordinate_system#:~:text=positions%20directly%20on%20Earth%20as%20latitude%20and%20longitude) and [compass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass#:~:text=a%20device%20that%20shows%20the%20cardinal%20directions%20used%20for%20navigation%20and%20geographic%20orientation)🧭directions are angles and thus can --- and should --- be measured in turns instead of [rad](#rad){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="radians"} or [°](#deg){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="degrees"}.

# Longitude latitude course {.hiddenheading #sec-llc}

Dec measures [longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitude#:~:text=denoted%20by%20the%20Greek%20letter%20lambda) in [wěi](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E7%B7%AF#:~:text=(geography)-,latitude,-coordinate%20terms%C2%A0%E2%96%B2) ([w](#w){#wei .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="horizontal lines on a map"}), [latitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latitude#:~:text=denoted%20by%20the%20Greek%20lower%2Dcase%20letter%20phi) in [jīng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meridian_arc#Full_meridian_(polar_perimeter):~:text=The%20polar%20Earth%27s%20circumference%20is%20simply%20four%20times%20quarter%20meridian) ([j](#j){#jing .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="jīng"}), and compass directions in [roses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass_rose#:~:text=a%20polar%20diagram%20displaying%20the%20orientation%20of%20the%20cardinal%20directions)🌹([r](#r){#rose .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="roses"}). To measure certain kinds of angles, Dec uses specific types of turns with distinct names like [w](#w){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="horizontal line on a map"}, [j](#j){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="jīng"}, or [r](#r){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="rose"}. All turn types can be combined with [metric prefixes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_prefix#:~:text=a%20unit%20prefix%20that%20precedes%20a%20basic%20unit%20of%20measure%20to%20indicate%20a%20multiple%20or%20submultiple%20of%20the%20unit), like [deci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deci-#:~:text=a%20decimal%20unit%20prefix%20in%20the%20metric%20system%20denoting%20a%20factor%20of%20one%20tenth), [centi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centi-#:~:text=a%20unit%20prefix%20in%20the%20metric%20system%20denoting%20a%20factor%20of%20one%20hundredth), or [milli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milli-#:~:text=a%20unit%20prefix%20in%20the%20metric%20system%20denoting%20a%20factor%20of%20one%20thousandth), to create turn [submultiples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_%28mathematics%29#:~:text=of%20%22a%20being-,a%20unit%20fraction,-of%20b%22%20%28a), such as [deciturns]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="tenths of a turn"} ([dt](#dt){#deciturn .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="deciturns"}), [centiturns]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="hundredths of a turn"} ([ct](#ct){#centiturn .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="centiturns"}), or [milliturns]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="thousandths of a turn"} ([mt](#mt){#milliturn .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="milliturns"}).

# Distance speed duration {.hiddenheading #sec-dsd}

Dec measures distance in [taur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turn_(angle)#Tau_proposals:~:text=%E2%81%A0%20turn-,Circumference%20of%20a%20circle,-%F0%9D%90%B6) ([c](#c){#taur .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="taur"}), speed in [omegar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angular_velocity#:~:text=linear%20velocity%20is%20the%20radius%20times%20the%20angular%20velocity) ([v](#v){#omegar .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="omegar"}), and time in years ([y](#y){#year .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="years"}) and days ([d](#d){#day .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="days"}). Each of these four turn types approximates ($\approx$) a physical property of the Earth🌍: [c](#c){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="taur"} = [$\underline{\tau r}$](#c){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="taur"} $\approx$ its [circumference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth%27s_circumference#:~:text=the%20distance%20around%20Earth), [y](#y){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="year"} $\approx$ the duration of its [orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth%27s_orbit#:~:text=From%20a%20vantage%20point%20above%20the%20north%20pole%20of%20either%20the%20Sun%20or%20Earth%2C%20Earth%20would%20appear%20to%20revolve%20in%20a%20counterclockwise%20direction%20around%20the%20Sun) around the Sun️, [d](#d){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="day"} $\approx$ the duration of its [rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth%27s_rotation#:~:text=the%20rotation%20of%20planet%20Earth%20around%20its%20own%20axis) on its [axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_tilt#:~:text=the%20imaginary%20line%20that%20passes%20through%20both%20the%20north%20pole%20and%20south%20pole), and $\text c\over\text d$ = [v](#v){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="omegar"} = [$\underline{\omega r}$](#v){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="omegar"} $\approx$ the speed of its rotation at the [Equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator#:~:text=the%20circle%20of%20latitude%20that%20divides%20Earth%20into%20the%20Northern%20and%20Southern%20hemispheres).

At a speed of [0.5]{.color5} [v](#v){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="omegar"} or [500]{.color5} [milliomegar]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="thousandths of an omegar"} ([mv](#mv){#milliomegar .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="milliomegar"}), we could travel the [0.1]{.color1} [c](#c){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="taur"} or [100]{.color1} [millitaur]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="thousandths of a taur"} ([mc](#mc){#millitaur .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millitaur"}) between the default positions📍of Points [0]{.point0} and [1]{.point1} in [0.2]{.color2} [d](#d){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="days"} or [200]{.color2} [millidays]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="thousandths of a day"} ([md](#md){#milliday .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millidays"}). The time required to travel between two points is the distance divided by the speed: \${distance_mcHsl0} [mc](#mc){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millitaur"} &divide; \${velocity_vHsl0} [v](#v){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="omegar"} = \${traveltimeHsl0} [md](#md){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millidays"} = \${distance_cHsl} [c](#c){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="taur"} &divide; \${velocity_vHsl1} [v](#v){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="omegar"} = \${distance_mcHsl1} [mc](#mc){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millitaur"} &divide; \${velocity_mvHsl} [mv](#mv){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="milliomegar"} = \${traveltimeHsl1} [d](#d){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="days"}.

Apart from [c](#c){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="taur"}, Dec also measures distance using a unit called the **z**one **e**quatorial **m**eter (zem). # Perpetually setting sun {.hiddenheading #sec-pss}

In [Slovak](https://sk.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zem)🇸🇰, [zem](#z){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="zone equatorial meter"} means Earth. This is fitting because all Dec units are based on physical attributes of the Earth. At the Equator, the Earth rotates on its axis at a speed of [~]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="approximately"}1.00224 [v](#v){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="omegar"}. If we could indefinitely maintain this speed while flying West in an airplane✈️towards the setting sun️, we would be able to perpetually fly [into the sunset](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/RidingIntoTheSunset)🌅.

# Airplane cruising speed {.hiddenheading #sec-acs}

To travel fast enough for a perpetual sunset, the airplane would need to surpass the [speed of sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_of_sound#:~:text=the%20distance%20travelled%20per%20unit%20of%20time%20by%20a%20sound%20wave)🔊([sos](#sos){#speedofsound .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="speed of sound"}), which at 15 [°](#deg){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="degrees"} [Celsius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celsius#:~:text=the%20unit%20of%20temperature%20on%20the%20Celsius%20temperature%20scale) and 1 [standard atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_atmosphere_(unit)#:~:text=a%20unit%20of%20pressure%20defined%20as%20101325%20Pa) is 0.735048 [v](#v){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="omegar"} or Mach 1. [Mach numbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mach_number) are relative to the [sos](#sos){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="speed of sound"}, which varies greatly by air temperature and pressure. The cruising speed of a [Boeing 747](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747#:~:text=sweep%2C%20allowing%20a-,Mach%C2%A00.85,-%28490%C2%A0kn;%20900) is [~]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="approximately"}0.54 [v](#v){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="omegar"} or Mach [~]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="approximately"}0.85.

The highway🛣️speed of a car🚗is roughly tenfold slower than the cruising speed of an airplane️. If we are driving on a highway at a speed of 50 [mv](#mv){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="milliomegar"} and our exit is 1000 [z](#z){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="zem"} away, we will have 20 [centimillidays]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="hundred thousandths of a day"} ([cmd](#cmd){#centimilliday .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="centimilliday"}) until we have to exit the highway️. To ensure we do not miss our exit, we can periodically check a countdown of the remaining [z](#z){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="zem"}: \${zLeft}.

# Centimilliday (cmd) {.hiddenheading #sec-cmd}

Dec refers to [cmd](#cmd){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="centimillidays"} as [beats]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="hundred thousandths of a day"} ([b](#b){#beat .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="beats"}) because they are similar in duration to heart❤️beats or [musical beats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beat_(music)#:~:text=I-,n%20music%20and%20music%20theory%2C%20the%20beat%20is%20the%20basic%20unit%20of%20time,-%2C%20the). In Dec, 1 [d](#d){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="day"} = 100 [centiday]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="hundredths of a day"} ([cd](#cd){#centiday .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="centidays"}) = [10^5^]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="a hundred thousand"} [b](#b){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="beats"} = [10^6^]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="a million"} [microdays]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millionths of a day"} ([µd](#ud){#microday .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="microdays"}), 1 [mc](#mc){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millitaur"} = 100 [kilozem]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="thousands of zem"} ([kz](#kz){#kilozem .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="thousands of zem"}) = [10^5^]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="a hundred thousand"} [z](#z){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="zem"} = [10^6^]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="a million"} [decizem]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="tenths of a zem"} ([dz](#dz){#decizem .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="decizem"}) = [10^6^]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="a million"} [nanotaur]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="billionths of a taur"} ([nc](#nc){#nanotaur .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="nanotaur"}), and therefore, 1 [mv](#mv){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="milliomegar"} = $\text{mc}\over\text d$ = $\text {kz}\over\text {cd}$ = $\text z\over\text b$ = $\text {dz}\over\text{µd}$ = $\text {nc}\over\text{µd}$. A [cd](#cd){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="centiday"} is [96]{.color960}% of a quarter hour and a [b](#b){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="beat"} is [86.4]{.color864}% of a second.

# Frequency period wavelength {.hiddenheading #sec-fpw}

Dec uses [þ](#per){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="perbeat"}, [b](#b){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="beats"}, and [z](#z){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="zem"}, often with metric prefixes, to measure the [frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency#:~:text=the%20number%20of%20occurrences%20of%20a%20repeating%20event%20per%20unit%20of%20time), [period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frequency#:~:text=the%20reciprocal%20of%20the%20frequency), and [wavelength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavelength#:~:text=the%20distance%20over%20which%20the%20wave%27s%20shape%20repeats), respectively, of a sound or light wave. Equations -@eq-freq, -@eq-peri, and -@eq-wave below show how frequency, period, and wavelength are related to each other and to speed. The speed of light ([sol](#sol){#speedoflight .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="speed of light"}) is [~]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="approximately"}647.551657 [kiloomegar]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="thousand omegar"} ([kv](#kv){#kiloomegar .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="kiloomegar"}), which is [~]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="approximately"}881 thousand times faster than the [sos](#sos){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="speed of sound"}.

:::{style="overflow-x:auto;overflow-y:hidden;"}
$$\text{frequency} = \dfrac{\text{speed}}{\text{wavelength}} = \dfrac{1}{\text{period}}$$ {#eq-freq}

$$\text{period} = \dfrac{\text{wavelength}}{\text{speed}} = \dfrac{1}{\text{frequency}}$$ {#eq-peri}

$$\text{wavelength} = \text{speed} \ast \text{period} = \dfrac{\text{speed}}{\text{frequency}}$$ {#eq-wave}
:::

The frequency range of the  [visible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visible_spectrum#:~:text=the%20band%20of%20the%20electromagnetic%20spectrum%20that%20is%20visible%20to%20the%20human%20eye) spectrum of light is [~]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="approximately"}328.32 to [~]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="approximately"}682.56 [teraperbeats]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="trillions of perbeats"} ([Tþ](#Tpb){#teraperbeat .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="teraperbeats"}). The range of sound frequencies which can be [audible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hearing_range#:~:text=the%20frequency%20range%20that%20can%20be%20heard%20by%20humans)👂for humans is [~]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="approximately"}[10.368](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hearing_range#:~:text=humans%20can%20hear%20sound%20as%20low%20as%2012%C2%A0Hz) to [~]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="approximately"}[24192](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hearing_range#:~:text=8%5D%20and-,as%20high%20as%2028%C2%A0kHz,-%2C%20though%20the%20threshold) [þ](#per){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="perbeats"}. The period and wavelength that correspond to the frequency chosen by the range input below are 1000 &div; \${iobs} [þ](#per){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="perbeats"} = \${parseFloat((1000 / iobs).toFixed(3))} [millibeats]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="thousandths of a beat"} ([mb](#mb){#millibeat .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="millibeats"}) and 735.048 [mv](#mv){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="milliomegar"} &div; \${iobs} [þ](#per){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="perbeats"} = \${parseFloat((735.048 / iobs).toFixed(3))} [z](#z){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="zem"}.

# Decioctave octave note {.hiddenheading #sec-don}

In addition to [þ](#per){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="perbeats"}, the limits of human hearing can also be expressed in [decioctaves]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="tenths of an octave"} ([do](#do){#decioctave .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="decioctaves"}). A [do](#do){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="decioctave"} is a tenth of an [octave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octave#:~:text=an%20interval%20between%20two%20notes%2C%20one%20having%20twice%20the%20frequency%20of%20vibration%20of%20the%20other) ([o](#o){#octave .tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="octave"}). An [o](#o){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="octave"} is a two-fold change in frequency. The audible range for humans is [~]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="approximately"}[3]{.color3} to [~]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="approximately"}[103]{.color3} [do](#do){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="decioctaves"}. The [range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Range_(music)#:~:text=the%20distance%20from%20the%20lowest%20to%20the%20highest%20pitch%20it%20can%20play) of an 88-key piano🎹is [~]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="approximately"}23.76 to [~]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="approximately"}3616.64 [þ](#per){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="perbeats"} or [~]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="approximately"}[8]{.color8} to [~]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="approximately"}[80]{.color0} [do](#do){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="decioctaves"}. # US customary units {.hiddenheading #sec-ucu}

Dec redefines [US](#us){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="United States"} customary units to facilitate [conversion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conversion_of_units#:~:text=the%20conversion%20of%20the%20unit%20of%20measurement%20in%20which%20a%20quantity%20is%20expressed) with [SI](#si){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="International System of Units"} and Dec units. The values in the first column of the tables below are approximate [fold changes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fold_change#:~:text=measure%20describing%20how%20much%20a%20quantity%20changes%20between%20an%20original%20and%20a%20subsequent%20measurement) from original to redefined [US](#us){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="United States"} customary units. A fold change of [1]{.color0} means [0]{.color0} [change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_change#:~:text=compare%20two%20quantities%20while%20taking%20into%20account%20the%20%22sizes%22%20of%20the%20things%20being%20compared) is required for alignment with the [SI](#si){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="International System of Units"} and Dec units shown in the second and third column, respectively.

Unlike Dec and [SI](#si){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="International System of Units"}, the [US](#us){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="United States"} customary measurement system does not use metric prefixes to scale units by [powers of ten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_of_10#:~:text=any%20of%20the%20integer%20powers%20of%20the%20number%20ten). Redefined [US](#us){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="United States"} customary units can serve as convenient reference points and provide intuitive names for certain fractions and multiples of Dec and [SI](#si){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="International System of Units"} units. For example, after being redefined, one hand is equal to a decimeter or a quarter [z](#z){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="zem"}.

# Summary {.unnumbered #tldr}

This article introduces the Dec measurement system and describes how Dec uses the properties of the planet Earth to define units based on turns for geographic coordinates, compass directions, distances, speeds, areas, volumes, weights, frequencies, wavelengths, and periods. Dec units can be combined with metric prefixes to form multiples or submultiples.

Dec attempts to bridge the gap, improve interoperability, and facilitate conversion between the [US](#us){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="United States"} customary and [SI](#si){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="International System of Units"} measurement systems by redefining certain units. Redefinition of [US](#us){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="United States"} customary units makes human-based length units [~]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="approximately"}[1.58]{.color0158}% shorter and surveying distance units [~]{.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="approximately"}[0.58]{.color0058}% shorter. Dec also redefines [SI](#si){.tool data-bs-toggle="tooltip" data-bs-title="International System of Units"} seconds to be [4]{.color040}% shorter.

Dec color labels can convey an impression of a value at a glance👀. Dec sound labels allow us to quickly estimate a value without even having to look at it🙈. Both types of labels can help avoid confusion when [decimal separators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decimal_separator#:~:text=In%20English%2Dspeaking%20countries%2C%20the%20decimal%20point%20is%20usually%20a%20small%20dot%20%28.%29%20placed%20either%20on%20the%20baseline) appear, disappear, or move due to a measurement unit change such as the addition, removal, or replacement of a metric prefix.
